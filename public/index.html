A journal for two

Our Journal is a private health journal for a couple. Log your weight, add a run straight from your watch picture, tick off daily habits and see how both of you are doing on one screen. It works on any phone, installs like an app and keeps working without internet.

Meals journal

Log a whole plate with one tap

Add the foods you usually eat once, with their calories, carbs, protein and fat per 100 g. Put them together into meal sets, like rice, fried fish and vegetables, and say which meals each is for, so breakfast only offers breakfast. Then pick the set, check the grams and add it to breakfast, lunch, dinner or a snack. Weigh a food a few times and the journal learns your usual portion, so the grams fill themselves in. A chart shows each day's calories against a range set from your BMI and what you burn, and skipped days are simply left out.

Dearly diary

Ledger

Spend together, and never pay your card too early

Log what you both spend in a few taps: the amount, the shop, and the category fills itself in from the shop name. Each person card shows where this month's money went. For a credit card with a rebate that depends on the statement balance, Ledger shows the balance building toward the next tier, the rebate so far, and how close each cap is. Type a card payment and it warns you before you pay too much too early and drop a tier, with how much is safe to pay now and when to pay the rest. When the statement comes, pick its PDF: it's read on your phone, never uploaded, and checked line by line against what you logged, so you can fix what's different in one go. Change a shop's category once and Ledger offers to remember it. Spending trends show each month by category, this statement against the last one, and your top shops, for one of you or both. Rebate rules are yours to set in a simple editor (tiers, rates, shared caps, what never earns), and a common Malaysian fuel card comes ready to use.

Ledger · money plan

Know what's left after the bills

Split your monthly income into buckets, like 55% essentials, 5% fun, 10% debt, 15% savings and 15% investing, and change the split any time. Add what you pay every month: house, car, loans, PayLater. Your money month starts on payday (the 25th, or any day you pick), so Ledger shows where this month's salary goes, what you can still spend, and how much that is a day until the next payday. Your day-to-day spending counts too. Tick each bill when it's paid. Give an instalment its final payment month and Ledger counts down the payments left. Tap "Analytics" for the next 12 months: what's free after bills each month, and the month each instalment ends and frees up money. A bucket that goes over its share turns red. Tap a bucket to see what it went on, month by month, and tap a shop to see everything you've ever spent there, so you know where cutting back or waiting for a sale would help. Adding spending comes first, so a coffee or some petrol takes seconds, and the month sits below as small tiles: what you can still spend, bills to pay and your card rebate. Tap a tile and it pops up over the page with everything in it. Tap "Arrange cards" to move any card up or down and make it small, medium or large, each size showing what fits. Your layout follows you to any phone you sign in on. Tap the paperclip on anything you logged or paid to add the receipt, now or weeks later: a photo or any file goes straight to your own OneDrive, named by date, shop and amount and filed by month.

    Questions

    What is Our Journal?

    A private web app for two people to track weight, BMI, running and daily habits together. It runs in the browser and can be installed on a phone like an app.

    How does it read my run from a picture?

    You choose the screenshot of your COROS run. Text recognition runs on your own phone to pick out distance, time, pace and heart rate. The picture is never uploaded or saved, and you check the numbers before saving.

    Which BMI ranges does it use?

    The WHO adult ranges: under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 is normal, 25 to 29.9 is overweight and 30 or more is obese. It also shows the lower Asian adult cut-off, where above 22.9 counts as overweight.

    Does it work without internet?

    Yes. Once opened, it keeps working offline, and new entries sync when the connection comes back.

    How does the mood card work?

    Each person taps one of five faces to rate how yesterday felt, from hard to great, and gets a short quote to match. The app never guesses your mood. A mood picked on a diary entry counts too, and several in a day are averaged. The Mood history page shows a year in pixels where tapping a day shows what happened that day and how many hard to great days that month (or week) had, reflection cards that look back over your days, and what tends to go with your good days, like exercise or habits.

    How does the meals journal work?

    You add the foods you usually eat once, with calories, carbs, protein and fat per 100 g, from the pack or a nutrition website. Then you save plates you eat often as meal sets. To log a meal, pick the set, check the grams and add it. After you weigh a food three times, the journal fills in your usual portion. Mark a set Cooked, list what goes in the pot from Stocky's Price book, and it shows about what a plate costs; mark it Bought and keep what a plate costs outside. A chart compares each day's calories with a range from your BMI and an estimate of what you burn, or the burn from your watch if you type it in. Days you don't log are left out, not counted as zero. A Year view colours one square per day, from far under to far over, with days logged, percent in range, average and best streak. The round button at the bottom switches between journals.

    How do I learn my way around?

    Tap the round button, then ⋯, then Show me how. Search for what you want to do and start a walkthrough. The app dims the screen, lights up each thing to tap and points at it with a dotted arrow, one step at a time. You can stop whenever you like.

    How does the Dearly diary writer work?

    Jot a few words about your day, in English, Malay, Manglish or a mix, and the writer turns them into a diary entry you can edit before saving. On a computer it can run inside the browser, using its graphics chip, so your notes are never sent to any AI company and there is no cost. You download the model once (about 0.3 to 1.7 GB) and it needs a browser with WebGPU. On phones, where that is too heavy, it uses your family’s own free cloud writer instead, and you can always write yourself.

    How does the Ledger money plan work?

    You type your monthly take-home income and split it into buckets that add up to 100%: essentials, fun, debt repayment, savings and investments to start, which you can rename and resize. Then add what you pay every month, with its bucket, amount and due day, and for an instalment its final payment month. Each month Ledger lists the bills by due date so you can tick them off, shows where the income goes, what you can still spend and how much that is a day, and turns a bucket red when it goes over its share. Day-to-day spending you log in Ledger counts too: dining and shopping as fun, the rest as essentials. The Analytics page shows the next 12 months: how much is left after bills and when each instalment ends. Tap any bucket to see what it went on: the shops and categories for this month or all months, for one of you or both, with a search. Tap a shop to follow it over time: all you've spent there, a usual month, a usual visit and the last 12 months. Ticks are typed by hand for now; checking them against a statement comes later.

    How does the Ledger work?

    Each of you logs spending with the amount, the shop and the card or wallet; the category fills itself in from the shop name. Each person card shows this month by category. For a credit card whose rebate depends on the statement balance, Ledger works out the balance so far, which rebate tier it reaches and how much rebate that is, with any caps. It assumes the last statement is paid by its due date. When you type a card payment before the statement date, it warns you if the payment would drop you a tier, says roughly how much rebate you would lose, how much is safe to pay now, and when to pay the rest. After each statement, you can pick its PDF: it is read on your phone and never uploaded, matched against what you logged, and the differences are listed so you can accept them together. If the PDF has no text, you can paste the lines instead. Change a shop's category and Ledger offers to remember it. Spending trends show each month by category, this statement against the last, and your top shops, and the rebate rules for any card can be edited. Logging is optional, and the numbers are a guide: your bank statement has the final say.

    How does receipt scanning work?

    Take a photo of a shop receipt in Stocky, or tap the receipt button on the Android home-screen widget, and the receipt is read for you: each item, how many and what it cost. Stocky matches each line to your things at home and remembers its pick for that shop next time, so you only choose once. It checks the lines add up to the printed total and asks you to look when they don't. You check every line, then save, and the buys feed the days-left guess and the price comparison. The photo is not kept.

    Can anyone sign up?

    Not at the moment. This journal is private to one family, and only they can sign in. Anyone can open the demo to try every feature with sample data.

    How is development progress tracked?

    Every change to the app is recorded in its history. On each release, that history is turned into the Development progress section: a wheel of how much work each journal has had and a dotted calendar of each day's work. Changes to docs, tools or the whole app at once are left out, and days before tracking began are shown as not tracked rather than as zero.

    How are visitors counted?

    Each phone or computer that opens this page is counted once, under the country its time zone points to. No location, IP address or personal detail is looked up or saved, only a number per country.

    Is anything I do in the demo saved?

    No. The demo uses made-up sample data kept only in your browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server, and it is all gone when you close the tab.

    See it for yourself

    The demo opens the full journal with two made-up people and 12 weeks of sample data. Add entries, tick habits, try every button. It all stays in this browser tab and is gone when you close it.
